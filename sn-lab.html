Parcel Sequence Optimizer

Practical problem: six parcels arrive in a mixed order. The dispatch desk must arrange them by destination priority. A permutation describes how their positions change.

Solved example: arrange six parcels

Priority: Islamabad → Karachi → Lahore → Multan. Preserve arrival order inside each destination group.

Initial arrival order
P1 (Lahore), P2 (Multan), P3 (Karachi), P4 (Lahore), P5 (Islamabad), P6 (Multan)
Required dispatch order
P5 (Islamabad), P3 (Karachi), P1 (Lahore), P4 (Lahore), P2 (Multan), P6 (Multan)
Permutation of original positions
π = (5, 3, 1, 4, 2, 6)
In two-line form: top 1 2 3 4 5 6; bottom 5 3 1 4 2 6
Inversions
(5,3), (5,1), (3,1), (3,2), (4,2) = 5 inversions.
Therefore minimum adjacent swaps = 5.

Answer: 5 adjacent swaps are sufficient to reach the required dispatch order.

Mathematical meaning

A permutation in Sₙ rearranges n positions. An inversion is a pair that is in the opposite order from the target sequence. The inversion count equals the minimum number of adjacent swaps needed to sort the sequence.

The destination priority is a chosen dispatch rule for this simulation. This is not a live warehouse conveyor controller.
